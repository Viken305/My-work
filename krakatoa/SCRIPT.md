# The Loudest Sound Ever Heard: narration and fact sheet

Voice: Kokoro `af_heart` (US female, 1.05×). Narration lines live in `script.py`. Years and numbers are written out in
words there so the voice reads them correctly ("eighteen eighty-three"); the captions show digits again (`DISPLAY`).

1. In 1883, police on a tiny island in the Indian Ocean heard what sounded like distant cannon fire. But there were no
   ships. The sound had come from almost 5,000 km away. From here.
2. Sailors on a ship 64 km away wrote that the blast burst the eardrums of half the crew. 160 km away, a pressure gauge
   at a gas works recorded a huge spike. From it, scientists estimate the sound there was about 172 decibels. Louder
   than a jet engine up close.
3. And it kept going. For 4 hours, the sound crossed the ocean. That was the cannon fire on Rodrigues, almost
   5,000 km away. No sound in recorded history has been heard that far.
4. But most of the wave was too low for any ear to hear. Barometers around the world picked it up. The pressure jumped
   again, and again, about every 34 hours, for 5 days. The wave went around the whole planet, three to four times in
   each direction.
5. Back at Krakatoa, most of the island was gone. Tsunamis over 30 metres high struck the coasts. About 36,000 people
   died. Ash spread around the world. For months, sunsets glowed blood red. Then, in 1927, something rose from the sea
   in the same place. Anak Krakatau. The child of Krakatoa. And it is still active today.

## Fact sheet

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| Police on Rodrigues heard it as distant guns | The chief of police on Rodrigues logged "the distant roar of heavy guns" (Royal Society report, 1888; widely quoted) |
| ~4,800 km away, about 4 hours later | Rodrigues is ~4,780 km from Krakatoa; the sound arrived about 4 hours after the blast |
| Final blast just after 10 am, 27 Aug 1883 | ~10:02 local time is the usual figure |
| Ship 64 km (40 miles) away; "eardrums of over half my crew" | Captain of the *Norham Castle*, as widely quoted; position shown on the map is illustrative |
| 160 km away, gas works pressure gauge spike; ~172 dB | Batavia gas works gauge jumped by >2.5 inHg, which converts to ~172 dB. **An estimate**, said as "about" |
| Louder than a jet engine up close | Jet engine at close range ~140–150 dB |
| Farthest sound in recorded history | Commonly cited; Rodrigues is the most distant place it was heard |
| Wave too low to hear (infrasound) | Most of the energy was below human hearing; barographs recorded it |
| About every 34 hours for 5 days; 3–4 times around each way | Barographs worldwide recorded passes ~34 h apart for ~5 days; up to 7 passes per station |
| London passes (on the barograph card) | Krakatoa–London ~11,700 km; with a ~34 h lap the passes fall near 10, 24, 44, 58, 78, 92, 112 h (7 in 5 days). The trace is drawn from this model, not a scan |
| Most of the island gone | Over two-thirds of the island collapsed into the caldera |
| Tsunamis over 30 m | Estimates 30–40+ m |
| About 36,000 died | Official toll 36,417, mostly from the tsunamis |
| Red sunsets worldwide for months | Britannica and many contemporary reports |
| Anak Krakatau rose in 1927, still active | First emerged 1927; collapsed partly in 2018; still erupts |
