# India video: style guide (draft v1)

Same visual language as the minerals and volcano videos (flat vector, soft gradients, glow, grain, constant gentle motion, the hard-hat bird), with a palette and motifs drawn from India.

## 1. Palette

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Night background | Deep indigo | `#141238` |
| Background alt | Royal purple | `#2B1C5C` |
| **Saffron** (flag, highlights, India's "accent") | Saffron | `#FF9933` |
| **India green** | Green | `#138808` (UI tint `#3FBF6B`) |
| **Chakra navy** | Navy | `#1A2A8C` |
| Marigold | Warm yellow | `#FFC233` |
| Peacock | Teal | `#1FB5A8` |
| Terracotta | Brick (Indus cities, Mughal red sandstone) | `#C8553D` |
| Turmeric | Gold | `#E8A317` |
| Rani pink | Festival pink | `#E5407E` |
| Marble | Taj white | `#F4EFE6` |
| Sombre (Partition, famine) | Desaturated slate | `#5B5870` |

**Rules**
- Each era gets a dominant tint: **ancient** = terracotta + turmeric; **empires** = red sandstone + marble; **colonial** = muted slate-blue; **freedom** = saffron sunrise; **republic** = flag colours; **culture** = full festival palette; **today** = peacock + saffron on indigo.
- Partition and famine scenes drop saturation and slow the motion. **Tragedy gets a quieter visual tone**: no jokes or bouncy animation.
- One glowing accent per shot, as before.

## 2. Motifs and transitions
- **Rangoli wipe:** a symmetrical rangoli pattern blooms from the centre to cover the frame and then dissolves into the next scene (main chapter transition).
- **Ashoka Chakra (24 spokes):** used as a turning-wheel transition into the Republic chapter and as the "time passing" clock.
- **Lotus, peacock feather, diya lamp:** small ambient accents (dust particles replaced by tiny floating marigold petals in culture scenes).
- **Chapter cards:** a pill badge like the volcano/minerals "RECIPE" cards, but numbered with Devanagari-style numerals next to English (१ 1 · ANCIENT INDIA).

## 3. Characters
- **The bird** (series mascot) is the curious guide. It reacts with "?" to questions, walks with Gandhi's salt-march crowd, wears a tiny cricket helmet in Part 5 and taps a UPI QR code in Part 6.
- **People:** simple, rounded, faceless figures in the series style, showing India's diversity (sari, kurta, turban, hijab, jeans, school uniforms, farmers, coders) in many skin tones. **No stereotypes, no caricatures.**
- **Historical and political figures** (Ashoka, Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Bose, Ambedkar, Modi):
  - Shown as **respectful flat portrait cards**: recognisable silhouette and attire, plus a name label and years.
  - **No exaggerated features, no caricature, no mocking or flattering poses.**
  - **Modi gets the same card style as every other figure**: neutral expression, same size and same background.
- **Atoms and magma blob** are not used here; this is a human story.

## 4. Maps
- India appears as a **clean stylised silhouette** with soft glow, built from simplified geographic data.
- **Decision needed (see below):** which boundary to draw in Kashmir and Arunachal Pradesh.
- Historical maps (Ashoka's empire, Mughal extent, British India, 1947 partition) are drawn as **soft coloured regions** over a faint modern outline, labelled with years, never as precise borders.

## 5. Typography and on-screen text
- Montserrat ExtraBold for labels and captions (as in previous videos).
- For script variety in the languages scene: Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Gurmukhi and Urdu (Noto fonts). The word shown is a simple greeting/"India" in each script.
- Captions name **sources of numbers** where helpful (e.g. "IMF, 2026 PROJECTION").

## 6. Sound
- Same procedural pad and plucks, retuned toward an Indian feel: a **drone (tanpura-like)** under the pad, scales based on raga-like modes (e.g. Bilawal/major for celebration, Bhairav-flavoured for the ancient scenes), and soft **tabla-style** percussion clicks for rhythm in the culture and today chapters.
- Partition: music drops to drone only.
- Narration: the temporary Kokoro voice, as before. You can record your own later; for an India video, a narrator with an Indian English accent would be a good choice.

## 7. Political balance (Part 6)
- Facts in the narrator's voice; opinions always attributed.
- **"SUPPORTERS SAY" and "CRITICS SAY" panels are identical in size, colour weight, number of points and time on screen.**
- No music stings or visual cues that make one side look good or bad.
