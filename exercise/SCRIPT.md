# What Happens Inside Your Body During Exercise: script and fact sheet (Beat Drop)

| Level | Narration | Fact behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Press Play | The moment you start exercising, your body turns into a rhythm game. Every organ has to hit its notes, right on time. Here is what happens inside, level by level. | (hook) |
| 1 · Ignition | Within seconds, your brain fires a signal, and adrenaline floods your blood. | The sympathetic nervous system switches on at the start of exercise, and the adrenal medulla releases adrenaline (epinephrine). |
| | Your heart speeds up, and the blood vessels in your muscles open wide. | Vasodilation in working muscle. |
| | At rest, your muscles get about twenty percent of your blood. Go hard, and that jumps to eighty percent. | Skeletal muscle gets about 15–20% of cardiac output at rest and up to 80–85% at maximal exercise. |
| 2 · Air | Your breathing goes from about twelve breaths a minute, to forty or more. | Breathing is about 12–20 breaths a minute at rest and 40–50 in intense exercise. |
| | Air flow jumps from six litres a minute, to around one hundred. | Minute ventilation is about 6 L/min at rest and 100+ L/min at maximal effort. |
| | Your blood grabs that oxygen, and races it straight to your muscles. | Haemoglobin oxygen transport. |
| 3 · Fuel | Muscles run on a tiny molecule called ATP, but they only store enough for a few seconds. | Stored ATP lasts about 2–3 s of maximal effort. |
| | So your body burns sugar and fat to recharge it, nonstop. | ATP is resynthesised from glycogen/glucose and fatty acids. |
| | When oxygen can't keep up, acid builds up in your muscles. That's part of the burn. | Anaerobic glycolysis raises hydrogen ions (acidosis), which contributes to the burning feeling. Lactate itself is not the cause. |
| 4 · Max Heart | Push hard, and your heart can hit around one hundred and eighty beats per minute. | Maximum heart rate is about 220 minus age, so about 180 at 40 and higher for younger people. |
| | Pumping up to twenty litres of blood every minute. That's four times more than at rest. | Cardiac output is about 5 L/min at rest and 20–25 L/min at maximum. |
| 5 · Heat | But muscles are messy engines. About seventy five percent of their energy becomes heat. | Muscle mechanical efficiency is about 20–25%, so the rest becomes heat. |
| | So your core temperature climbs, and your skin can sweat more than a litre an hour. | Core temperature reaches about 38–39 °C, and sweat rates are 1–2 L/h in hard exercise in the heat. |
| Bonus | Then comes the bonus round. Your brain releases endorphins and endocannabinoids, the chemicals behind the runner's high. | Both rise with sustained exercise. Endocannabinoids are now thought to drive much of the runner's high. |
| | And after you stop, your body keeps burning extra calories while it recovers. | EPOC ("afterburn"). |
| | Train often, and your heart gets stronger, so it needs fewer beats at rest. | Endurance training enlarges stroke volume and lowers resting heart rate. |
| | Level complete. Hit play again tomorrow. | (loop back to the play button) |

## Sources

- Fuss et al., 2015, *PNAS*, "A runner's high depends on cannabinoid receptors in mice"; Siebers et al., 2021, *Psychoneuroendocrinology* (humans).
- Børsheim & Bahr, 2003, *Sports Med*, "Effect of exercise intensity, duration and mode on post-exercise oxygen consumption".
