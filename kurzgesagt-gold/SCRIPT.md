# How Earth Makes Gold (script)

**Format:** vertical 9:16 Short · **Guide:** AURI, a gold atom · **Length:** about 2:40


## 1. Hook

> **H1** The gold in a wedding ring is older than the Earth. Older than the Sun.
>
> **H2** It was made in one of the most violent events in the universe. And getting it into your hand took billions of years.

## 2. The forge

> **F1** Stars can fuse atoms into heavier ones, but only up to iron. To build something as heavy as gold, you need something far more extreme.
>
> **F2** Two neutron stars, the collapsed cores of dead stars, spiral into each other and collide.
>
> **F3** For a split second, atoms are flooded with neutrons, and heavy elements like gold are forged. Scientists think a single collision can make several Earths' worth of gold.

## 3. The lost gold

> **L1** That gold drifted through space and ended up in the cloud that formed our solar system.
>
> **L2** But when the young Earth was molten, heavy metals sank. Almost all of Earth's original gold fell into the core, where it still is today.
>
> **L3** There's enough down there to cover the whole planet in a layer about four metres deep.

## 4. The delivery and the squeeze

> **D1** So why is there any gold up here at all? Scientists think much of it arrived later, delivered by meteorites crashing into the young Earth.
>
> **D2** Even then, it was spread thin through the rock. Then hot water, deep underground, picked it up and carried it through cracks.
>
> **D3** When an earthquake suddenly opens a crack, the pressure drops, the water flashes into steam, and gold is left behind in a vein.

## 5. The punchline

> **O1** All the gold ever mined by humans would fit in a cube about twenty two metres wide.
>
> **O2** So the next time you see something golden, remember: it was forged when stars collided, lost to the core, rained down from space, and squeezed out by earthquakes.
>
> **O3** Not bad for a little piece of metal.
