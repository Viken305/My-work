## Fact check

| Claim | Basis | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine enters the blood within about 10 minutes; nearly all of it (~99%) is absorbed by about 45 minutes | Fast absorption, mostly in the small intestine | Institute of Medicine (2001), *Caffeine for the Sustainment of Mental Task Performance*, ch. 2; Blanchard & Sawers (1983), *Eur J Clin Pharmacol* 24:93 |
| Adenosine builds up while you are awake and drives sleepiness | Sleep pressure | Porkka-Heiskanen et al. (1997), *Science* 276:1265 |
| Caffeine looks like adenosine and blocks its receptors | Caffeine is an A1/A2A adenosine receptor antagonist; both share the purine double ring (shown on screen) | Fredholm et al. (1999), *Pharmacol Rev* 51:83 |
| Adrenaline rises and blood pressure ticks up | Caffeine raises plasma epinephrine and causes an acute rise in blood pressure | Robertson et al. (1978), *NEJM* 298:181; Mesas et al. (2011), *Am J Clin Nutr* 94:1113 |
| Dopamine lifts mood | Caffeine boosts dopamine signalling indirectly (A2A–D2 receptor interaction; raised D2/D3 receptor availability) | Volkow et al. (2015), *Transl Psychiatry* 5:e549 |
| Nearly 1 in 3 people get the urge to go | 29% of 99 healthy volunteers reported coffee triggered the urge to defecate (an effect of coffee, not of caffeine alone) | Rao et al. (1998), *Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol* 10:113 |
| Half-life of about 5 hours | Average adult half-life is about 5 h (range roughly 1.5–9.5 h; longer in pregnancy, shorter in smokers) | Institute of Medicine (2001); Nehlig (2018), *Pharmacol Rev* 70:384 |
| A big dose 6 h before bed can cut about an hour of sleep | 400 mg taken 6 h before bedtime reduced total sleep time by more than 1 hour | Drake et al. (2013), *J Clin Sleep Med* 9:1195 |
| The crash | When caffeine clears, the adenosine that kept building up binds freely again, which gives rebound tiredness. This is a simplified model of a widely described effect. | Fredholm et al. (1999) |
| Under about 4 cups a day for most adults | FDA: up to 400 mg a day is not generally linked to negative effects in healthy adults, which is about 4–5 cups of brewed coffee. Lower limits apply in pregnancy. | U.S. FDA, "Spilling the Beans: How Much Caffeine Is Too Much?" (2018, updated 2024) |

Visual simplifications: the clock times on screen (sip at 8:00 AM, chart times) are illustrative. The "1 HR" sleep bar shows the measured loss, not someone's whole night.
