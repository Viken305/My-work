# Earth Is Weirder Than You Think (script)

**Guide character:** NIMBUS, a little cloud who drifts around the world  
**Length:** about 4:16 (8 parts)


## 1. Hook

> **H1** You've lived on this planet your whole life. You know it. Blue oceans, green forests, solid ground.
>
> **H2** But Earth is far stranger than it looks. And our guide today is someone who has seen all of it from above.
>
> **H3** This is Nimbus, a little cloud who drifts around the world. Let's go see what's really going on down there.

## 2. More trees than stars (chapter 1)

> **T1** Look up on a clear night, far from any city, and the Milky Way seems endless. It holds somewhere between one hundred and four hundred billion stars.
>
> **T2** Now look down. Scientists estimate there are around three trillion trees on Earth.
>
> **T3** That's more trees on our planet than stars in our galaxy. Many times more.

## 3. Not a perfect ball (chapter 2)

> **B1** Earth isn't a perfect ball either. Because it spins, it bulges out at the middle, like a slightly squashed orange.
>
> **B2** The equator sits about twenty one kilometres farther from the centre than the poles.
>
> **B3** So the highest mountain isn't the point closest to space. The summit of Chimborazo in Ecuador, near the equator, is about two kilometres farther from Earth's centre than the top of Everest.

## 4. The runaway Moon (chapter 3)

> **D1** Even a day isn't what it used to be. About one point four billion years ago, a day on Earth lasted less than nineteen hours.
>
> **D2** The Moon is to blame. Its gravity pulls on our oceans, and that tidal friction slowly brakes Earth's spin.
>
> **D3** In return, the Moon drifts away from us, about three point eight centimetres every year. Roughly as fast as your fingernails grow.

## 5. Lightning & ice (chapter 4)

> **L1** Right now, somewhere on Earth, lightning is striking. In fact, it strikes about forty four times every second, more than a billion times a year.
>
> **L2** And the largest desert on Earth isn't hot at all. It's Antarctica.
>
> **L3** A desert is defined by how little rain or snow falls, not by heat. Parts of Antarctica get less precipitation than the Sahara.

## 6. The desert that feeds a rainforest (chapter 5)

> **S1** Speaking of the Sahara, it wasn't always a desert. Between about eleven thousand and five thousand years ago, it was green, with lakes, grasslands, hippos and crocodiles.
>
> **S2** Today, its dust still helps keep another place alive. Every year, winds carry millions of tonnes of Saharan dust across the Atlantic Ocean.
>
> **S3** Much of it falls on the Amazon rainforest, delivering phosphorus, a nutrient its soils badly need. The world's biggest desert helps feed the world's biggest rainforest.

## 7. The real majority (chapter 6)

> **A1** And you're never far from the planet's true majority. Scientists estimate there are around twenty quadrillion ants on Earth.
>
> **A2** That's roughly two and a half million ants for every single person.
>
> **A3** Put them all on a scale, and together they would weigh about a fifth as much as all of humanity.

## 8. Still unexplored (outro)

> **O1** And here's maybe the strangest fact of all. Only about a quarter of the ocean floor has been mapped in detail.
>
> **O2** Most of our own planet's surface is still a mystery, hidden under kilometres of dark water.
>
> **O3** So the next time you think you know this world, remember: Earth still has plenty of secrets left.
