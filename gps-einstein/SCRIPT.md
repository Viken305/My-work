# Your Phone Does Einstein's Math: narration

Voice: Kokoro `af_heart`, 1.05×, en-us. Numbers are spoken as words; captions show digits.

## 1. Hook: Einstein in your pocket

- **H1** Right now, your phone is doing Einstein's math.
- **H2** Without it, the blue dot on your map would drift off by about ten kilometres, every single day.
- **H3** So what does Einstein have to do with finding a coffee shop?

## 2. Clocks in the sky

- **A1** Twenty thousand kilometres above you, about thirty satellites circle the Earth.
- **A2** Each one carries atomic clocks, and keeps shouting the same message. Here's where I am, and here's the exact time.
- **A3** Your phone hears that message a split second later. That delay tells it how far away the satellite is.

## 3. Four satellites, one dot

- **B1** One satellite puts you somewhere on a giant sphere. Add two more, and the spheres narrow it down to a single point. That's you.
- **B2** A fourth satellite fixes your phone's own clock, because a phone clock is never exact.
- **B3** And the timing has to be perfect. Light travels thirty centimetres in a billionth of a second. Be off by a millionth, and you're off by three hundred metres.

## 4. Einstein's problem

- **C1** Here's the problem. Those clocks don't tick at the same speed as ours.
- **C2** They fly at fourteen thousand kilometres an hour, and Einstein showed that moving clocks run slow. Seven microseconds a day.
- **C3** But up there, gravity is weaker. And weaker gravity makes time run faster. Forty five microseconds a day.
- **C4** Add them up, and the satellite clocks gain thirty eight microseconds, every day.

## 5. The fix and the payoff

- **D1** So engineers slow them down before launch. On the ground, they tick a tiny bit too slow. In orbit, they tick exactly right.
- **D2** Without that fix, every map in the world would be about ten kilometres wrong by tomorrow.
- **D3** Relativity isn't just a theory. It's in your pocket, every time you check where you are.
